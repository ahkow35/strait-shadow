    A submarine stealth game

    Strait Shadow

    Illustrative model. Interior layout is not public. Fictional scenario.

    You command a Type 218SG-style submarine. Formidable-class frigates and their Seahawk helicopters hunt you. Listen, stay quiet, find a firing position, shoot, and slip away.

    Mission
    Endless

    Difficulty

    Turn your phone sideways

    Strait Shadow is laid out for a phone held sideways: the boat gets the middle of the screen and every control fits along the bottom.

    Nothing happens when you turn it? Rotation lock may be on: swipe down from the top-right corner and tap the lock.

    How to play

    Tabletop controls

    • Steer. Drag a finger across the plot. A dashed line from the boat shows the new course; let go and the boat turns onto it. The solid line is where the bow points now. With a torpedo on its wire the line turns red when the turn would cut the wire: slow down first, or turn in smaller steps.
    • Look around. Pinch, or press − and +, to zoom. Drag with two fingers to move the chart; Centre brings it back to the boat.
    • Contacts. Tap a contact, or its bearing line, to make it the target: the strip reads what sonar knows about it. Tap the strip to open its Solution card, with − and + to hold a course or a speed by hand. Tap open water to clear the target.
    • Torpedoes. Set, beside Fire, opens the torpedo settings. While a torpedo is on its wire the seeker view shows what it hears, with Next, Retarget and Reject below it. With several on the wire, tap one on the plot to choose which takes the orders. A drag on the plot only ever sets your own course.
    • Depth and speed. Shallower and Deeper move the ordered depth 10 m at a time, or drag the gauge between them. The − and + beside the speed set the throttle.
    • The boat in 3D. Tap the small picture, or 3D, for the full view; Plot brings the table back. Scope looks through the periscope.
    • The top bar. Tap the mission name for the objective, and the damage picture for the damage panel. The ≡ menu holds pause, time compression, the mast, the bearing history, this help, sound and quit. The game does not wait while the plot is open: use Pause.

    Each mission states its goal at the top left. A frigate takes two torpedo hits. Your own boat can be holed, flooded and set on fire: see Damage control.

    • Listen. The ring shows where sounds come from: the top of the ring is your bow. A bigger dot is a louder, closer contact. You hear less when you go fast, and nothing straight behind you. Slow and quiet, you hear much farther: the Hearing bar under the noise meter shows it.
    • Classify. A new contact is Unknown. Keep listening and the sound signature panel fills in its shaft rate, then its blade count, then its class. Fishing boats and merchant ships are neutral: hitting one costs a star, ends the mission on Hard, and costs 150 points in Free Patrol. With an unknown or neutral contact ahead, Fire must be pressed twice. A look through the periscope classifies a ship at once.
    • Sea life. Whales, dolphins and schools of fish are sonar contacts too, and they also start Unknown. Listening finds no shaft rate, only irregular sounds, and classifies them as Biologic; a whale is heard only while it sings. Hold fire: a torpedo that goes off among whales or dolphins costs a star, ends the mission on Hard, and costs 150 points in Free Patrol. With sea life ahead, Fire must be pressed twice.
    • Shrimp beds. Snapping shrimp crackle over every shoal: the map dots their beds. Over one, anything that only listens hears you far less well, and you hear far less yourself. A ping, yours or theirs, is not affected.
    • Stay quiet. Speed makes noise, steeply above Half. Going fast near the surface is louder still. Silent running halves your noise but holds you at Slow and stops reloading. Quiet is strong, not perfect: an active ping, a dipping sonar or a sonobuoy close by can still find you.
    • Use the layer. The dashed line on the depth gauge is a layer of colder water. Below it the frigate's hull sonar hears you poorly. The helicopter lowers its sonar through the layer, so it still can.
    • Plot. The map draws each sound as a bearing line. Bearings taken on a steady course give no range. Change course and fire control works out a solution: where the contact is, its course and its speed. The ellipse shows how unsure the position still is, and the arrow is the course fire control predicts. A ping or a look through the periscope gives a fix at once, and gives you away. Drag the map to look around; Centre brings it back to your boat. The small strip on the map is the bearing history: every sound leaves a trace, newest at the top, so a trace that slides sideways is a contact whose bearing is changing.
    • Solution. Fire control grades each solution None, Poor, Fair or Good, and gives the range, course and speed with their likely error. The strip of dots under them is the bearing residuals, measured less predicted: dots along the middle line mean the solution fits. If you know better, hold a course or a speed by hand; Auto hands it back, and Restart fit drops the old bearings. A grade is fire control's own opinion: it can be wrong, most of all just after the target turns, so watch for "Target may have turned". If the dots scatter, the target may have turned: restart the fit before firing. Turning to sharpen a solution can cost you the wire.
    • Shoot. Choose a target: Fire then aims the torpedo by its solution, ahead of the target. With no target, or no solution yet, it runs along the bow. The torpedo settings are chosen before the launch and cannot be changed after it. Swim-out is quiet but slow to start; Impulse is fast and loud. Enable is how far it runs before its seeker listens. A Deep run is quieter, and passes under ships until it climbs. Search is what it does when it finds nothing: carry straight on, snake, or circle back once. A torpedo arms 300 m from the tube (a game value): nearer than that it passes harmlessly.
    • On the wire. The torpedo trails a wire. The seeker view shows what it hears ahead of its nose, and what it has locked on to: "moving, machinery" is a ship; "stationary, no machinery" is how a noisemaker sounds, and sea life reads "moving, no machinery". Reject makes it leave that lock and search again; Retarget sends it to the selected contact, listening to nothing on the way. It can be steered left or right while it runs out or searches. A hard turn, more than Half speed, or a long run breaks the wire, and then the weapon is on its own.
    • Missile. Two tubes start with an anti-ship missile, and two more wait in the rack. A missile reaches 9 km, far beyond a torpedo, and flies there in under a minute. It has no wire: it goes where the solution says the ship will be, then its own radar picks the ship nearest that point, neutral or not. It needs a selected contact with a solution graded Poor or better (a rough aim is enough, because its radar searches for itself) that puts the ship between 1.5 and 9 km, and the boat no deeper than 60 m and no faster than 6 kn. Update missile (Z, or the Update button on the plot) sends every missile in flight the current solution, but only while the boat is at periscope depth with the mast up, where it can be seen. A missile that had locked lets go and searches again from the new aim point. The flight is short, so have the mast up before you fire if you mean to update. Route, in Settings, is Direct or a Dog-leg to the left or right: a dog-leg flies out to one side and turns in, which takes about 20 s longer and needs the ship at least 5 km off (closer than that it flies direct, and says so). It comes in from a side the ship is not watching, so even an alert frigate is slow to react. The launch flame still shows them where the boat is. Swapping a tube between torpedo and missile takes 60 s and no other tube reloads meanwhile; silent running pauses it. Sea-skim stays very low and is seen late, but meets the ship's gun. Pop-up climbs, is seen early and gives the ship's missile defence more time, but dives steeply, is harder for the gun and strikes amidships more often. A hit amidships does twice the damage of a hit forward or aft; a hit forward puts the ship's gun out of action. A frigate defends itself with its missile defence, its gun and chaff decoys, jams the missile's radar once it is alert, which makes it lock a little later, and turns bow-on; two missiles together are harder to stop. The launch breaks the surface with a flame: it ends silent running, and about 15 s later they search where it came up. All of these are game values.
    • Search depth. A torpedo set Shallow searches near the surface for ships and passes over a submarine below it. Set Deep, it searches at one depth chosen at launch from the contact's layer note, then follows its target up or down. Fire control suggests Deep when the target is a submarine.
    • The Archer. In Silent Hunt, and sometimes in Free Patrol, an Archer-class submarine hunts you. It is about as quiet as you are and listens as well; it creeps, stops to listen and sprints. Nothing tells you when it has heard you, so listen for it first, stay slow and quiet, and set your torpedo Deep.
    • Slip away. When a torpedo is in the water, release a noisemaker, turn away and go deep and quiet. You carry four (a game value).
    • Damage control. A torpedo hit holes one of the four sections, shakes the whole boat and may start a fire; so does hitting the seabed fast, or a ship's hull overhead at periscope depth. The damage panel shows each section: water in blue, fire in orange, smoke in grey, the injured as a number, and a marker where the damage-control team works. Water comes in faster the deeper you are, and the pumps are weaker deep, so go shallower and keep some speed: the planes hold up a heavy boat. Send the team with 1 to 4 or a tap; it puts out fire first, then plugs the hole, then pumps. A burning section can be sealed once: the fire dies, but everyone inside is hurt. Below the red crush line on the depth gauge the hull gives way. As a last resort the emergency blow (B twice) drives the boat to the surface: loud, and only once.
    • Stars. Each mission gives up to three stars, and the title screen says what earns them. Every mission is open from the start, in any order. In Free Patrol frigates keep coming, and the score is kept.

    Fire control, the torpedo settings and the seeker view are illustrative game mechanics, not how any real system works. All numbers are game values, not real specifications. Anti-ship missiles are a 'what if': reports say this class of boat may be able to fire them from its torpedo tubes, but it is not confirmed. The ship's defences and decoys, the damage by hit location, the swap time and the alarm a launch raises are game choices.
